This is a working revision of the original draft. Every [TO BE CALCULATED] that could be computed from the actual Set1_535 raw data has been filled in with a real number, and every figure that could be produced from real files has been embedded below. Four things must be resolved with the research team before this goes further — they are not data-entry gaps, they are places where the draft's own assumptions conflict with what the files show:

  1. Only 535 images exist, not 685. No 150-image drone/aerial subset was supplied. Every number in this revision covers the 535-image Set1_535 release only. If the drone subset exists elsewhere, it must be added and this whole document re-run before the "dual-modality" framing can be published as written.
  2. The 535 images do not look like independent mobile-phone photographs. All 535 files carry zero EXIF data (confirmed three separate ways: Image.getexif(), Image._getexif(), and the raw file headers, which show bare JFIF, not EXIF). Filenames cluster into exactly three timestamps, all on 2026-04-16, and the embedded frame number increases by a near-constant 10 across consecutive files — the signature of frames extracted from a short video, not 535 separate photos. This resolution (7680×4320 / 4320×7680) matches the sibling AerialYield-B2D blueberry paper's video_frame modality exactly, not its smartphone modality (3000×4000).
  3. The real annotation data has 6 ripeness classes, not 4. The COCO JSON and mask folders define Green, Breakers, Turning, Pink, Light Red, Red — the full USDA/Silal six-stage scheme — not the draft's proposed 4-class merge. Both the 6-class real numbers and a 4-class collapse (matching the draft's original Table 1) are given below; which one ships is a scientific decision for the authors, not something this revision can make.
  4. The proposed split leaks. A grouped, leakage-safe split (exactly what §"Split-generation strategy" below specifies) has not yet been generated. A naive stratified split was tested instead, and 46.6% of adjacent same-session frame pairs land in different train/val/test subsets — real leakage risk given finding 2.

An independent, read-only audit of the working repository (not of this manuscript) additionally found geometry defects in the raw COCO annotations (908 self-intersecting polygons, some duplicate annotations) that affect how confidently "9,260" can be called a validated instance count — see the note in the COCO/instances section below.

Abstract

Tomato harvest decisions in protected cultivation depend on visual maturity judgements made across whole trusses rather than on isolated fruit, yet publicly available tomato image resources are dominated by single-modality, close-range collections that do not represent the wider canopy views produced by aerial scouting platforms. We present AerialYield-T²M, where T²M denotes the Tomato Two-Modality dataset, a curated real-image resource of 685 RGB images of greenhouse tomato plants. The release combines 535 close-range images acquired with mobile-phone cameras and 150 wider-view images acquired with a drone or extracted from drone video. [...]

Real-data statusOnly the 535-image subset exists in the working repository at the time of this revision, and (per finding 2 above) its acquisition modality reads as video-frame extraction, not mobile-phone photography. The abstract's "535 mobile + 150 drone" framing cannot be confirmed as written until (a) the 150-image subset is supplied and (b) the acquisition method for the 535-image subset is confirmed with whoever collected it.

Confirmed numerical facts, this revision: 535 images in the working Set1_535 release; 9,260 COCO-annotated tomato instances across 6 ripeness classes (real 4-class collapse given in Table 7 for comparison). The 150-drone-image figure and the 685 total remain unconfirmed against actual files — see finding 1.

Background & Summary

Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) is among the most intensively cultivated horticultural crops in protected environments, and the timing of its harvest is governed almost entirely by visual maturity. The commercial reference framework is the colour-based classification codified by the United States Department of Agriculture, which partitions red-fleshed cultivars into green, breakers, turning, pink, light red and red on the basis of the proportion of the fruit surface that has departed from green [1]. This scheme is deliberately perceptual: it is defined in terms of what a grader sees, which makes it directly transferable to image-based systems but also makes it sensitive to illumination, viewing angle and partial visibility.

Preprocessing Pipeline

(The 11-step pipeline description is unchanged and matches what the working code actually does, with one correction:)

Correction to step 14The draft says per-class instance counts come "from the annotation export, not from connected-component analysis of the masks" — this is correctly implemented. However, the independent connected-component cross-check mentioned in the same step has a bug in its current form: it measures foreground pixels inside each component's bounding-box slice rather than pixels matching that exact label, which lets neighbouring components inflate each other's measured area. An independent audit recomputed this properly and found the current code reports 19,159 components; the corrected per-label computation gives 19,157 — two different numbers that were previously conflated as one. Neither should be called "the instance count"; COCO annotations (9,260) are the instance count.
